Fees · Upwork

Upwork fees explained for clients and freelancers

Last checked: Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · Official Upwork Help · Not a quote or invoice

Upwork fee conflict (flagged). Upwork’s client pricing page (checked Nov 5, 2026) leads with Basic 5% (or 3% for eligible U.S. checking-account payers) and Business Plus 10% (or 8% with the bank discount), plus Contract Initiation Fees. Some FAQ/schema blocks on the same page still mention 7.99%. We treat the hero/examples 5% / 10% as the current public headline and mark 7.99% as conflicting; your checkout is authoritative.
Read this first. Upwork fees depend on client plan, billing method, geography, contract type, and the freelancer’s locked service-fee % for that contract. Connects, memberships, Contract Initiation Fees, withdrawal fees, FX, and taxes sit outside the headline %. Always re-check the offer / billing screen before you hire.

Client Marketplace Fee (Basic plan)

Upwork’s Client Marketplace Fee article states that on the free Basic plan, clients pay 5% (pricing-page headline) on payments to freelancers, including fixed-price and hourly contracts, Project Catalog, bonuses, Direct Contracts, consultations, payroll, expenses, and miscellaneous charges.

  • Eligible U.S. clients using a bank account as billing method may pay a reduced fee of 3%.
  • The fee is not refundable, even if a payment is reversed or refunded.

Business Plus client fees

Upwork’s Business Plus fees article states:

  • 10% on all freelancer payments (hourly, fixed-price, Project Catalog, bonuses).
  • Eligible U.S. clients paying with a checking account may see a reduced 8% fee.
  • A one-time Contract Initiation Fee of up to $4.99 on new contracts under $100 USD. Active Business Plus clients are not charged that initiation fee on hourly contracts.
  • Changing Business Plus membership updates billing immediately for open and new contracts.
  • Net 30 monthly invoicing (application required) has no extra fee per that article.

The monthly subscription price of Business Plus itself is not restated here — confirm on Upwork’s current membership / pricing UI before budgeting membership cost.

Freelancer Service Fee

Upwork’s Freelancer Service Fee article states the fee ranges from 0% to 15% per contract. Key mechanics:

  • You see the % when a client sends an offer or when you submit a proposal.
  • Once the contract begins, the fee is fixed for that contract.
  • If money is refunded to the client, Upwork returns the Freelancer Service Fee paid on that amount.
  • Exceptions / special cases called out officially include Upwork Payroll, Any Hire (no Freelancer Service Fee), Direct Contracts (reduced fee — see Upwork’s Direct Contracts article), and Enterprise (often ~10%, but customized).

There is no single “everyone pays 10%” rule anymore — read the % on the offer screen.

Worked examples (illustrative)

Assume a $1,000 fixed-price payment, no FX, no tax, no Connects cost.

  • Basic client at 5%: client pays ~$50 marketplace fee on top of $1,000 to the freelancer.
  • Basic U.S. bank payer at 3%: client pays ~$30 fee on $1,000.
  • Business Plus at 10%: client pays ~$100 fee on $1,000 (or ~$80 at the 8% checking-account rate if eligible).
  • Freelancer at 10% locked fee: keeps $900 of $1,000 billed (before withdrawal / FX).
  • Freelancer at 5% locked fee: keeps $950 of $1,000 billed.

Client fee and freelancer fee both apply on the same engagement. They are not substitutes for each other.

Costs people forget

  1. Connects — freelancers spend Connects to propose; membership tiers change Connect pricing. Deep dive: Upwork Connects cost explained ($0.15 each on Help; Plus $19.99 / 100 Connects on pricing).
  2. Contract Initiation Fee — small contracts under $100 can add up to $4.99 for some plans.
  3. Withdrawal and FX — can exceed the headline % on cross-border payouts.
  4. Hourly vs fixed-price protection — different payment-protection rules; see escrow and milestones.
