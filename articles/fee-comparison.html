Cornerstone · Fees

Freelance platform fee comparison: Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer.com, Toptal, PeoplePerHour

Last checked: Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · Sources linked below · Outbound links go to official platform pages

Read this first. Platform fees change. Memberships, payment method, geography, contract type, FX, taxes, withdrawal fees, and optional upgrades can all move the real total. The table below is a starting map, not a quote. Always re-check the official fee page before you budget a hire.

At a glance

Headline marketplace / network fees (standard cases)
Platform What clients typically pay What freelancers typically pay Notes
Upwork Basic plan Client Marketplace Fee up to 7.99% on payments to freelancers; eligible U.S. bank-account payers may pay 3%. Business Plus listed at 10% (or 8% for eligible U.S. checking-account payers). Freelancer Service Fee 0%–15% per contract (shown before you accept; locked once the contract begins). Client fee is stated as non-refundable even if a payment is reversed. Connects/membership costs are separate.
Fiverr Service fee 5.5% of purchase amount, plus $3.50 on orders under $200. Each separate payment (extra, tip) gets its own fee. Freelancers earn 80% of the purchase amount (platform keeps ~20%), including extras and tips. Clearing period before withdrawal (help pages cite 14 days; shorter for some levels). Early Payout may add a fee.
Freelancer.com Fixed-price project fee 3% or $3 (whichever greater) at award; hourly 3% per payment. Optional upgrades extra. Fixed-price 10% or $5 (whichever greater); hourly 10% per payment. Preferred Freelancer Recruiter projects: 15%. Transaction, withdrawal, membership, verification, and arbitration fees can add cost. Milestone Payment System holds funds until release.
Toptal Published: $79/month platform subscription if you proceed with matching; talent billed at quoted rates (no public rate card). Trial of up to two weeks; unsatisfied means unbilled for that trial period per FAQ. Not published as a simple % marketplace fee. Talent compensation is arranged through Toptal. Do not treat third-party “average hourly rate” blogs as official. Ask Toptal for a written quote.
PeoplePerHour Buyer / payment-processing fees are omitted here until we confirm them from an official page. Service fee on earnings by lifetime billing per buyer: 20% below £250; 7.5% from £250–£5,000; 3.5% above £5,000 (excl. VAT). Min service fee £1/invoice. Bands are lifetime per buyer relationship, not global lifetime earnings.

How to think about “total cost”

  1. Separate client fees from freelancer fees. A 20% seller commission is not the same as a 5.5% buyer service fee — both can apply on the same order.
  2. Watch fixed add-ons on small jobs. Fiverr’s $3.50 under-$200 fee hits short logos harder than a $2,000 build.
  3. Membership and connects are not free. Upwork Connects and Freelancer memberships/upgrades change effective cost before a contract exists.
  4. Withdrawal, FX, and tax can exceed the headline % on cross-border work.
  5. Screened networks price differently. Toptal’s cost is mostly the talent rate you are quoted, not a transparent marketplace take-rate.

Worked examples (illustrative only)

Assume a $500 fixed-scope design job, no upgrades, no FX, no tax. These are arithmetic illustrations from the sourced headline rates — not invoices.

  • Fiverr: Client pays ~$500 + 5.5% + $3.50 ≈ $531. Freelancer receives 80% of $500 = $400 before withdrawal/clearing.
  • Freelancer.com: Client project fee max(3% of $500, $3) = $15. Freelancer fee max(10% of $500, $5) = $50.
  • Upwork: Depends on plan and the freelancer’s locked service-fee %. Example only: Basic client at 7.99% pays ~$39.95 on $500; a freelancer at 10% keeps $450 of $500 billed. Re-check the offer screen for the live %.
  • PeoplePerHour: On a first £500 relationship that crosses the £250 band mid-engagement, blended freelancer fees apply per the band rules — do not assume a flat 20%.
  • Toptal: $79 subscription + quoted talent rate. No honest single-% comparison without a quote.
