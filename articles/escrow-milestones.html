How escrow and milestone payments work on freelance platforms

Last checked: Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · Methodology: official help pages + product docs · Not legal advice

Plain English. Escrow (or “project funds” / “milestone payments”) means money is committed before or during work and released according to platform rules — not according to whoever argues hardest in chat. It reduces some payment uncertainty. It does not guarantee quality, perfect dispute outcomes, or recovery from every mistake.

The shared pattern

  1. Define a chunk of work with an amount attached (a milestone or fixed-price phase).
  2. Fund it so the freelancer can see the money is real before (or as) they start.
  3. Deliver and review inside a window the platform defines.
  4. Release, refund, or dispute according to written rules — not informal DMs.

Upwork (fixed-price / milestones)

Upwork’s help documentation describes fixed-price work funded through milestones held as project funds. Typical flow from official help:

  • Client funds a milestone before work on that milestone proceeds.
  • Freelancer submits work; client reviews.
  • Official help describes a client review window and what happens if the client does not respond — check the live Upwork article for the current window and hold times before you rely on exact numbers.
  • After approval, funds may sit in a short security/clearing hold before withdrawal availability.

Hourly contracts use a different model (tracked time, billing, Hourly Protection rules) — do not mix the two when advising a client.

Primary refs: How payments for milestones and fixed-price contracts work; Escrow for Fixed-Price Jobs; Fixed-Price Protection.

Fiverr (orders and optional milestones)

Most Fiverr Gigs are prepaid orders: the client pays at checkout (plus service fee), the freelancer delivers, and the order completes through Fiverr’s order/resolution flow. Custom offers and some packages support milestones, where Fiverr charges each milestone as the client approves and moves forward (Paying for orders…, checked Nov 5, 2026).

Payment protection here is platform-mediated order state, not a generic bank escrow product. Scope still has to be written into the Gig or custom offer.

Freelancer.com (Milestone Payment System)

Freelancer.com’s fees page states that for Services, employers fund the service price and payment is protected by the Milestone Payment System: release only when the employer is satisfied. For projects, milestone deposits sit until released, refunded, or handled under the User Agreement / dispute process. Arbitration of a milestone dispute is listed at $5 or 5%, whichever is greater, with a winner-refunds-fee rule (feesandcharges, checked Nov 5, 2026).

Toptal

Toptal is a screened talent network with invoicing, not a classic public marketplace escrow board. The FAQ describes a trial of up to two weeks and Net 10 invoicing. Treat payment protection as contractual/network process, not “fund a public milestone.” (toptal.com/faq)

PeoplePerHour

PeoplePerHour payment-protection / WorkStream details are omitted here until we confirm the current wording from official help pages. We will not invent mechanics.

What escrow does not fix

  • Vague scope (“make the brand pop”) that both sides can interpret differently.
  • Off-platform payment requests that abandon protection.
  • Wrong payment details, chargebacks, or identity fraud.
  • Subjective taste disputes with no acceptance criteria.
  • Silence — unless the platform’s rules define what silence does.

Direct-hire alternative: Trustless Network (contextual)

Ownership disclosure: Trustless Network is affiliated with the owner of this site. Its official site describes it as XRPL escrow for freelancers and Web3 work. Prefer marketplace escrow when you need large talent supply, card billing, or a traditional dispute desk. We are not expanding product claims here beyond that plain description.

Practical checklist before you fund anything

  • Written deliverables, file formats, and “done” definition.
  • Milestone amounts that match reviewable chunks.
  • Review window and what happens if nobody clicks.
  • Revision count and what counts as a revision vs. new scope.
  • Stay on-platform until release if you want that platform’s protections.
