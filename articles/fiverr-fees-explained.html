Fees · Fiverr

Fiverr fees explained for clients and freelancers

Last checked: Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · Official Fiverr Help Center · Not a quote or invoice

Read this first. Fiverr shows the exact total at checkout before you pay. Taxes, payment-method rules, memberships, Promoted Gigs, Seller Plus, Early Payout, FX, and withdrawal methods can move the real number. Re-check the linked help articles before you budget.

Client (buyer) fees

Fiverr’s help article on paying for orders states:

  • Standard service fee: 5.5% of the purchase amount.
  • Orders under $200: an additional $3.50 fee.
  • Every separate payment — including extras and tips — carries its own service fee.
  • Milestone-based custom offers / Gig packages charge each milestone separately as you approve and move forward.

That under-$200 fixed add-on is why a $50 logo Gig can feel expensive as a percentage of order value, while a $2,000 website package is mostly the percentage fee.

Freelancer (seller) fees

Fiverr’s earnings help page states that for every completed order, freelancers earn 80% of the purchase amount, including Gig Extras and tips (platform keeps the remaining ~20%). Amounts shown are before taxes.

  • Completed payments typically enter a clearing period before they become withdrawable. Help pages historically cite multi-day clearing; confirm the live clearing window in your account — we are not restating a fixed day count here without a current primary source for every seller level.
  • Eligible freelancers can use Early Payout to skip clearing for a 1% fee (per the earnings help page, checked Nov 5, 2026).
  • Withdrawal methods (PayPal, bank, etc.) may add processor fees outside Fiverr’s 20% commission — check the withdrawal article for your method.

Worked examples (illustrative)

Assume no tax, no FX, no Early Payout, one payment. Arithmetic from the sourced headline rates only.

$200$200 + $11.00 = $211.00 (no $3.50)$160

If the client later buys a $40 tip or extra, that payment gets its own 5.5% (+ $3.50 if that payment is under $200). The freelancer still earns 80% of that tip/extra amount.

When Fiverr’s fee shape fits (and when it doesn’t)

  • Fits: Packaged scope with clear deliverables, prepaid orders, clients who want a listed price and revision count.
  • Awkward: Tiny under-$200 jobs where the $3.50 dominates; long ambiguous projects better split into funded milestones elsewhere; buyers who need hourly time tracking as the primary model.
