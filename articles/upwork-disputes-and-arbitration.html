Upwork disputes and arbitration explained

Last checked: Nov 9, 2026 (America/Denver) · Official Upwork Help Center · Not legal advice

Short version. On fixed-price contracts, a dispute runs: refund request or unreleased milestone → freelancer files within 7 days → client responds within 5 days → free, non-binding Upwork mediation (typically within 2 days) → 2 days to accept → if rejected, a Notice of Non-Resolution and 7 days to opt into paid, binding arbitration by Brief. On hourly contracts, clients can dispute billed hours only, in a short window after each billing week.
What we could not confirm. Upwork does not publish a fixed arbitration price. It says the cost is shown in the email offer, usually split evenly, with Upwork paying part of it on contracts over $20,000. We do not quote a dollar figure. Upwork’s hourly dispute article also gives slightly different timing in different places (freelancer response “within three calendar days”, a review of “up to one business day”, and results “within seven calendar days”); we list each as written.

This page answers one question: once an Upwork payment is contested, what are the steps, deadlines and costs? For what Upwork protection covers in the first place (funded milestones, the desktop tracker, manual time), see Upwork Payment Protection.

Two details from Upwork Help matter in practice:

  • Evidence is one-shot. Both the freelancer’s dispute form and the client’s response say you cannot edit or add details after you submit. Attach the milestone description, messages and deliverables the first time.
  • Feedback first. On an ended contract, a freelancer must leave feedback for the client before the dispute form opens.
  • Active contracts. If the client will not end the contract or release payment, Upwork tells freelancers to contact Support with proof the work is complete; only the client can end the contract to trigger the refund-request route.

Arbitration: outcomes and cost

Arbitration is only offered on fixed-price contracts, by email, to parties who qualify. Brief, a third-party company Upwork says is not affiliated with it, makes a binding decision that Upwork upholds. Not complying with the outcome can lead to an account suspension that may block payments or withdrawals.

Cost: shown in the arbitration email; usually split evenly; Upwork pays part on contracts over $20,000. For a small milestone, compare the fee in that email with the amount at stake before you opt in.

Practical read for freelancers: because a mutual decline returns escrowed funds to the client, a freelancer who believes the work met the milestone has to be willing to pay for arbitration to keep the claim alive. For clients: ignoring a dispute for five days releases the requested funds to the freelancer.

Hourly disputes (clients)

  • Window: from Monday noon UTC until Friday 23:59 UTC after the billing week closes. Contacting Support or requesting a refund does not count as filing.
  • Where: the contract’s Timesheet tab → Dispute. You cannot dispute more hours than were billed that week.
  • Grounds: billed hours only. Quality complaints are not grounds for an hourly dispute.
  • Process: the freelancer has three calendar days to accept or decline; an Upwork specialist reviews the Work Diary against Hourly Protection criteria. Upwork says you will know the result within seven calendar days.
  • Withdrawing releases the funds to the freelancer, and you cannot re-file for that week.
  • Refund requests (not disputes) can be made for payments released up to 365 days ago; refunding is at the freelancer’s discretion.

Freelancers protect themselves on hourly work by meeting the tracker and memo rules listed on Upwork Payment Protection. Choosing between contract types: hourly vs fixed-price contracts.

Before it gets to a dispute

  • Write milestones that can be judged against a deliverable, so a mediator can compare the work with the description. General guidance: escrow and milestone payments.
  • Keep scope changes and approvals in Upwork messages; anything agreed off-platform is outside Upwork’s process (off-platform payment rules).
  • Clients writing a brief that holds up in review: hiring brief template (sister site).

FAQ

How long do I have to file a fixed-price dispute on Upwork?

Upwork Help (checked Nov 9, 2026) says a freelancer has seven calendar days to review the payment details and submit a fixed-price dispute after a refund request. If no dispute is filed in that window, the remaining funds are refunded to the client.

What happens if the other side ignores an Upwork dispute?

On fixed-price disputes, the client has five calendar days to respond. Upwork says that if the client does not respond in five days, the funds the freelancer requested are released to the freelancer.

Who pays for Upwork arbitration?

Arbitration is run by Brief, a third-party company. Upwork says the cost appears in its email offer, that the freelancer and client usually split it evenly, and that Upwork pays part of the fee on contracts over $20,000. Upwork does not publish a fixed price.

Can a client dispute hourly work because of poor quality?

No. Upwork's hourly dispute process covers billed hours only. Upwork says quality complaints are not grounds for an hourly dispute; the client can ask the freelancer for a refund instead.
