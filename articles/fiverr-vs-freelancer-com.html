Fiverr vs Freelancer.com fees for clients

Last checked: Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · Client fees only

Short version. Fiverr charges buyers 5.5% plus $3.50 on orders under $200. Freelancer.com charges employers 3% or $3 (whichever greater) on fixed-price awards. Freelancer.com’s published employer % is lower, but bidding upgrades, method fees, and freelancer take-rates still change totals.
Headline client / employer fees (checked Nov 5, 2026)
PlatformClient / employer feeNotes
Fiverr5.5% + $3.50 under $200Each payment (extra, tip) gets its own fee. Sellers separately keep ~80%.
Freelancer.com3% or $3 (greater) fixed; 3% hourlyOptional project upgrades; card/PayPal/Skrill $0.30+2.3% (non-AU). Freelancer fee 10%/$5 separate.

Worked examples (client/employer fee only)

  • $100: Fiverr $5.50+$3.50=$9.00. Freelancer.com max(3%,$3)=$3.00.
  • $500: Fiverr 5.5%=$27.50. Freelancer.com 3%=$15.
  • $2,000: Fiverr $110. Freelancer.com $60.

When the shape differs beyond the %

  • Fiverr is catalog/Gig-first with prepaid orders; good when scope fits a package.
  • Freelancer.com is bid/contest-heavy; employers may buy upgrades; freelancers need bid balance.

FAQ

Is Freelancer.com always cheaper for clients than Fiverr?

On published employer/buyer % alone, Freelancer.com’s 3% is lower than Fiverr’s 5.5%, especially under $200 where Fiverr adds $3.50. Upgrades, payment-method fees, and how freelancers price their bids can erase that gap.

Do freelancers pay the same on both?

No. Fiverr sellers typically keep 80% of the purchase amount. Freelancer.com freelancers pay 10% or $5 (greater) on fixed-price awards — a different mechanism.
