Wise vs PayPal for freelancers receiving payment

Last checked: Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · US-focused merchant/receive fees · Not tax advice

Short version. Wise’s US receive page states that getting local account details and receiving many domestic (non-Swift / non-wire) payments is free, with a fixed fee for some wires (e.g. $6.11 USD to receive USD wire/Swift). PayPal’s US merchant fees page (updated Nov 4, 2026) lists Send/Receive Money for Goods and Services at 2.99%, and standard PayPal Checkout / many commercial types at 3.49% + fixed fee ($0.49 USD among others), plus +1.50% on international commercial receives.
Marketplace warning. Moving a platform job to Wise or PayPal can void that marketplace’s payment protection and may violate off-platform rules. Read off-platform payment rules before you change rails mid-contract.

Wise Local account details free; domestic non-Swift/non-wire receives in listed currencies free. USD wire/Swift receive: $6.11 fixed (page table). Other Swift currencies have their own fixed fees. Converting or sending out has separate pricing.
PayPal (US business) Goods and Services send/receive: 2.99%. PayPal Checkout / many commercial: 3.49% + $0.49 USD fixed (currency table). International commercial: +1.50%. Instant withdrawals can add 1.50%. Currency conversion spreads (often 3–4%) when PayPal converts.

Worked $1,000 domestic US receive (illustrative)

  • Wise local/ACH-style receive: $0 receive fee on the published domestic free path → $1,000 credited (before any later convert/send fees).
  • PayPal Goods and Services 2.99%: ≈ $29.90 fee → about $970.10 net (before withdrawals/FX).
  • PayPal Checkout 3.49% + $0.49: ≈ $35.39 fee → about $964.61 net.

Protection and operational differences

  • PayPal Goods and Services is the commercial path with seller fees and PayPal’s purchase-protection framework for eligible goods/services disputes. Friends and Family is a different product — lower or no receive fee in many domestic cases, but it is the wrong tool for client work and can leave you exposed if a payment is reversed.
  • Wise is built around multi-currency account details and transfers; dispute/chargeback dynamics follow bank rails more than a marketplace escrow. It does not replace platform escrow.
  • Invoices: PayPal’s invoicing rates on the merchant fee page often mirror Checkout (3.49% + fixed) or card rates (2.99% + fixed) depending on how the buyer pays — read the invoicing section of the same fees page.

When each tends to fit

  • Wise: Clients who can pay local bank details; freelancers holding multiple currencies; minimizing receive % on large invoices.
  • PayPal: Clients who only pay wallets/cards; you need PayPal’s commercial tools and accept the % fee.
  • Neither: A substitute for reading the marketplace contract you already signed.

FAQ

Is Wise always free to receive?

No. Wise’s receive page lists free domestic paths for several currencies, but USD wire/Swift and many international Swift receives have fixed fees. Sending, converting, and some add-money methods have separate fees.

Should freelancers use PayPal Friends and Family for clients?

We advise against it for paid client work. Goods and Services is the commercial path. Friends and Family can remove purchase-protection expectations and create dispute risk if a payment is challenged.
