Upwork vs Freelancer.com: fees and hiring model

Last checked: Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · Client + freelancer fee shapes · Not a ranking

Short version. Freelancer.com publishes a simple employer 3% or $3 (greater) / freelancer 10% or $5 (greater) project fee. Upwork’s client pricing page (checked Nov 5, 2026) leads with Basic 5% (or 3% for eligible U.S. checking-account payers) and Business Plus 10% (or 8% with the bank discount), plus contract initiation fees. Freelancer take-rates on Upwork are a separate 0–15% Freelancer Service Fee locked per contract.
Upwork fee conflict (flagged). The same Upwork client pricing page still contains FAQ/schema text mentioning a 7.99% Basic Marketplace fee alongside the hero/FAQ examples that say 5%. We treat the prominently displayed 5% / 10% (and 3% / 8% bank discounts) as the current public headline and mark 7.99% as stale-or-conflicting until Upwork consolidates the page. Worked $500 fixed-price example (illustrative)

  • Freelancer.com: employer fee max(3%×$500, $3) = $15. Freelancer fee max(10%×$500, $5) = $50. Method fees / upgrades extra.
  • Upwork Basic at 5%: client marketplace fee ≈ $25 on a $500 payment to the freelancer, before any Contract Initiation Fee. Freelancer keeps $500 minus their locked Freelancer Service Fee % (example only — re-check the offer).
  • Upwork Basic at 3% (eligible U.S. bank): client fee ≈ $15 on $500, before initiation fee.

Hiring-model differences that change the fee math

  • Bidding: Freelancer.com free members start with a limited monthly bid allotment and must hold a minimum balance to bid. Upwork uses Connects for many proposals (priced separately; verify current Connects packs before budgeting).
  • Contests: Freelancer.com runs contest prizes with employer Money Back Guarantee rules; Upwork’s core marketplace is proposal/contract based, not contest prizes.
  • Protection: Both use milestone-style funding, but the review windows and hourly rules differ — read escrow & milestones and Upwork Payment Protection.

When each shape tends to fit

  • Freelancer.com often fits buyers who want a low published employer % and are comfortable evaluating many bids / optional upgrades.
  • Upwork often fits buyers who want Upwork’s escrow/hourly tooling and will trade a higher client % (especially Business Plus) for those workflows — verify the live % on the offer screen.

FAQ

Is Freelancer.com always cheaper for clients than Upwork?

Not always. Freelancer.com’s 3% employer fee is lower than Upwork Basic’s 5% headline, but Upwork’s 3% bank discount can match Freelancer.com’s 3% on the payment itself — and Contract Initiation Fees, upgrades, method fees, and freelancer take-rates still change the total. Compare the live checkout.

Do freelancers pay 10% on both?

Freelancer.com’s standard freelancer project fee is 10% or $5 (greater). Upwork’s Freelancer Service Fee is 0–15% locked per contract and is a different mechanism — do not assume 10%.
