Hourly vs fixed-price freelance contracts: which protects you better?

Last checked: Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · Sources: Upwork Help Center, Freelancer.com Fees and Charges

The one-sentence rule

Choose fixed-price when you can describe “done” in advance; choose hourly when the work is ongoing or scope is genuinely unknown — and then follow the platform’s tracking rules exactly, because hourly protection depends on them.

How fees land on Freelancer.com

  • Fixed-price: employer 3% or $3 and freelancer 10% or $5 (whichever is greater) are charged at award/acceptance, plus on any overage.
  • Hourly: 3% (employer) and 10% (freelancer) on each payment as it is made.
  • Milestone disputes can go to paid arbitration ($5 or 5%). See Freelancer.com fees explained.

Common failure modes

  • One giant milestone: 14 days is not enough to evaluate a whole app. Split it.
  • Unfunded “next milestone”: freelancers working ahead of funding have no escrow protection.
  • Hourly without a cap: clients get bill shock; set a weekly limit.
  • Scope changes in chat only: update the milestone or contract so the record matches the work.

FAQ

Is hourly or fixed-price safer on Upwork?

Each is protected differently. Fixed-price relies on escrow-funded milestones with a 14-day review window; hourly protection covers only time tracked in the Upwork desktop app that meets activity and weekly-limit rules (Upwork Help, checked Nov 5, 2026).

What are Upwork's contract minimums?

$3 per hour for hourly contracts and $5 for fixed-price projects.
