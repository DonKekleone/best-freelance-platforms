Guru.com fees explained

Last checked: Nov 7, 2026 (America/Denver) · Official Guru freelancer pricing page + employer Help

Short version. Freelancers pay a job fee on every paid invoice: 9% on Basic and Basic+, 7% on Professional, 6% on Business, and 5% on Executive. Paid plans are listed at $11.95 to $49.95 per month, or less per month when billed annually. An employer can contribute up to 5% toward that job fee.
Fee-share wording (flagged). Employer Help says the job fee ranges from 5% to 9% and that the employer may “split up to 5% of this job fee,” then lists three choices: the employer pays 2.5% and “the freelancer bears the remaining 2.5%,” the employer pays 5% and “the freelancer bears no job fee,” or the employer pays nothing and the freelancer bears the full job fee. The “remaining 2.5%” / “bears no job fee” wording only lines up cleanly with a 5% Executive fee, not with Basic’s published 9% fee. We do not invent a leftover percentage for other plans. Use the agreement screen.
Other Guru fees omitted on this check. Guru’s Terms of Service and the freelancer “Job Fee” Help article did not load for us automatically (HTTP 403). Those pages may list extra items such as payment-handling, transfer or arbitration fees. They are left out here until we can reconfirm them from the live pages, rather than copied from secondary write-ups.

The pricing page says you can save up to 27% when you pay annually. Memberships are described as non-transferable and non-refundable. You can renew starting two weeks before expiry; if a paid membership lapses, Guru says you are moved back to Basic.

Other published membership extras

  • One bid sends one quote. Premium Quotes are listed at 6 bids (Professional), 5 bids (Business) or 4 bids (Executive).
  • Sales Messages (starting a conversation with a potential employer) are listed at 5 / 4 / 3 bids on Professional, Business and Executive.
  • Only paying members can send direct messages, according to the pricing page.
  • Search Boost amounts listed on the table are $1,000 (Basic+), $2,000 (Professional), $4,000 (Business) and $8,000 (Executive). The page does not spell out in that table whether those figures are included credits or something else, so treat the live plan comparison as the detail source.

$500 invoice example (illustrative)

Assume a $500 invoice, no fee-share, no other add-on fees and no FX. These are arithmetic illustrations from the published job-fee percentages.

  • Basic or Basic+ at 9%: job fee $45; freelancer keeps $455 before any other costs.
  • Professional at 7%: job fee $35; freelancer keeps $465.
  • Business at 6%: job fee $30; freelancer keeps $470.
  • Executive at 5%: job fee $25; freelancer keeps $475.
  • If the employer elects to pay 5% of the job fee on an Executive agreement, Employer Help says the freelancer bears no job fee on that invoice. We do not extend that “zero fee” claim to a 9% Basic invoice.

FAQ

What is the Guru.com job fee?

Guru charges freelancers a job fee on every paid invoice: 9% on Basic and Basic+, 7% on Professional, 6% on Business, and 5% on Executive.

How much do Guru.com freelancer memberships cost?

Basic is free. Paid monthly prices on the freelancer pricing page are Basic+ $11.95, Professional $21.95, Business $33.95 and Executive $49.95. The same page lists lower per-month prices when billed annually: $8.95, $15.95, $24.95 and $39.95, and says you can save up to 27% annually. Memberships are described as non-transferable and non-refundable.

Can a Guru.com employer share the job fee?

Yes. The freelancer pricing page says the employer can contribute up to 5% toward the job fee, depending on the agreement and membership level. Employer Help lists three choices: the employer pays 2.5%, pays 5%, or pays none of the job fee.
